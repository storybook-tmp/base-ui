# Droppy brand guidelines

Purpose: How Droppy looks, sounds, and behaves — so anyone designing a new screen, writing
a new string, or building on the Base UI-based design system expresses the same brand,
whichever product they're working in.

## What Droppy is

Droppy is a food-delivery company, and the name of the design language every Droppy
product is built with (shipped as `@droppy/theme`). The company runs two products today:

- **MealDrop** — the consumer app. Browse restaurants near you, pick a meal, check out, done.
- **DropBoard** — the back office. Where restaurant partners manage menus, orders, and payouts.

This page defines the company-level brand: the personality, color system, typography,
voice, motion, and accessibility rules shared by everything Droppy ships. What's specific
to each product — MealDrop's mascot and wordmark, DropBoard's data-dense register — lives
in the [Product brands](#product-brands) section at the end. Everything above that section
applies to both.

Droppy is warm and unfussy: the kind of company whose software gets out of your way and
makes you smile on the way through. Nothing shouts — not even the primary button. A calm
mostly-grey interface, a voice that's casual and a little playful, and personality
concentrated in small moments rather than sprinkled everywhere.

## Personality

Four attributes, in priority order. When a design decision is ambiguous, the higher one wins.

- **Calm** — quiet, uncluttered, low-contrast. Grey does almost all the work. Content gets
  room to breathe; controls stay compact. Restraint is the default.
- **Friendly** — approachable and human, never corporate. Rounded forms, sentence-case copy
  that sounds like a helpful friend.
- **Playful** — small moments of delight and the occasional wink (yes, the Star Wars joke).
  Character is concentrated, not sprinkled everywhere.
- **Content-first** — the user's content is the star; the chrome recedes. In MealDrop that
  means the food does the tempting. In DropBoard it means the numbers stay legible.

## Color

Droppy's palette is a large neutral system with a few small, deliberate splashes of color.
The governing idea: grey does the work; color marks moments.

### The roles

1. **Neutrals — the workhorse** (~80% of every screen). The grey ramp carries every surface
   and almost all text, including the primary button. Ink/primary text: `#2C2C2C` ·
   Secondary text: `#636363` · Surfaces: white, `#F9F9F9` (cards), `#F5F6F7` · Dark
   surfaces/footer: `#2C2C2C`, `#202020`
2. **Teal — the identity color** (brand moments only). `#22ACA7` core, plus `#36C1BF` /
   `#4CC8C0` / `#61D8DE` / `#B1DCE3`. Teal is Droppy talking about itself — reserved for
   brand surfaces: the logo, the loading spinner, the places where the company expresses
   its identity. NOT for buttons, links, or interface chrome. (Dark mode: lime/green ramp.)
   In MealDrop the teal ramp lives in the mascot; in DropBoard, the wordmark and spinner.
3. **Soft green — the "good news" accent.** `#E5F8BC`. Marks progress and positive moments:
   promo/top banners, progress steps, "new" tags. In dark mode it also becomes the primary
   button. Used at only a handful of sites app-wide — if a screen has more than one green
   moment, one of them is probably wrong.
4. **Pale blue — the "calm" accent.** `#B1DDE4`. Hero/banner backgrounds, review-star
   text, and the focus ring. A quiet, restful accent.
5. **Red — errors only.** `#B71C1C` (base `#D70808`). The single expressive-but-serious
   color; used for error text and nothing decorative.

### Color principles

- **Grey does the work; color marks moments.** Reach for the neutral ramp first. Color is the
  exception that means something.
- **The accents are surfaces, not ink.** Soft green and pale blue are backgrounds that carry
  dark text — they're too light to draw a legible line, border, or icon. The deliberate
  exceptions are blue-only: the focus ring and review-star text, which use blue's dark end.
- **The brand color belongs to brand surfaces.** Teal is identity, not interface. Keeping it
  off the chrome is what lets the brand moments feel special.
- **Hierarchy is size and weight, not color.** Emphasize with a size step or a heavier
  weight — never by recoloring text. The one exception is error text.
- **Dark mode re-decides roles; it doesn't invert.** Roles are reassigned deliberately (e.g.
  the primary button becomes green; brand teal goes lime). Design both themes on purpose
  rather than tone-mapping one into the other.

## Typography

Two faces, each with a clear job — shared by every Droppy product.

- **Montserrat — display.** Headings and wordmarks. Weights 400/500/700/900.
- **Hind — text & UI.** Body copy, labels, and buttons. Weights 400/500.

Type scale (rem): h1 2.74 · h2 2.19 · h3 1.75 · h4 1.4 · body 1.125 · small 1 · xs 0.9 ·
xxs 0.72. (Root drops to 14px on small screens.)

Rules:

- **Hierarchy is size, not color.** All heading levels share the same ink; h1/h2 add tight
  tracking (−2px), h3/h4 don't. Emphasis within text is weight (medium/500) or a size step.
- **Buttons are body text with a background.** Button labels are set in Hind, regular
  weight — never the display face, never bold, never uppercase. A CTA earns attention through
  its fill, not its type.
- **Sentence case, always** (see Voice).

## Voice & tone

Droppy talks like a friendly, slightly cheeky human — never like a corporation. The
principles are company-wide; how much cheek is appropriate is a product decision (a 404 can
joke; a payout report shouldn't).

- **Sentence case everywhere.** Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only. Never Title
  Case, never ALL CAPS.
- **Short and direct.** "View all restaurants." "Complete order." "Your cart is empty." Say
  the thing.
- **Warm, with room for a wink.** Empty and error states are where personality lives — a
  light joke is welcome when it doesn't get in the way.
- **Punctuation with feeling.** A plain period for the everyday; an exclamation for genuine
  celebration or a real problem ("Order confirmed!", "Something went wrong!").

Product-specific voice examples live under [Product brands](#product-brands).

## Imagery & illustration

Illustration-first, across all products.

- **Character illustrations** — flat, friendly SVG scenes. Warm, simple, human.
- **Animated illustrations** — Lottie animations for error and 404 states. Motion here is
  part of the charm, not decoration.
- Keep illustration and photography in their lanes: illustration for personality and
  states, photography for content appeal.

## Motion

Motion is brief, functional, and calm — with character reserved for the brand.

- **Interactions run short and ease-in** (roughly 150–300ms). Buttons, hovers, transitions.
- **Theme switch cross-fades over 250ms** — a small, deliberate delight; keep it.
- **Character animation belongs to the brand marks.** Logo animations and branded spinners
  are Droppy being playful about itself — don't replicate that energy on ordinary controls.
- **Ambient motion is a marketing indulgence.** A slow hero pan is fine on a marketing
  surface; it doesn't belong inside a product's working screens.
- **Elevation means floating, not importance.** Shadows are for things that hover above the
  page (modals, sidebars, hover lifts). Resting cards stay flat, separated by a background
  shift, not a shadow.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the brand's calm, not an afterthought.

- **One focus ring, everywhere.** A single blue two-layer ring (thin ring + soft halo) is the
  focus treatment for every control, in every product. Don't invent per-component focus styles.
- **Legible by construction.** Because the green and blue accents are surfaces carrying dark
  text (never colored text on white), they stay readable by design. Keep them that way.
- **Never rely on color alone.** Hierarchy and meaning come through size, weight, and copy —
  color is a supporting signal, not the only one.
- **Sentence case aids scanning** and keeps the tone human. It's an accessibility choice as
  much as a style one.
- **Both light and dark themes are designed to meet contrast** on their own terms.

## Product brands

How each product expresses Droppy. Everything above applies to both; this is where the
product-specific identity lives. A product brand may add character on top of the design
language — it may not contradict it.

### MealDrop

The consumer food-delivery app — and the product where Droppy's playfulness runs warmest.

- **Mascot.** MealDrop's mark is a friendly creature — a two-eared fox built from soft SVG
  shapes. The mascot is where Droppy's teal identity ramp lives (`#22ACA7` core through
  `#36C1BF`, `#4CC8C0`, `#61D8DE`, with a pale-blue nose `#B1DCE3`; lime/green in dark
  mode). Don't recolor it into interface greys; don't put the ramp anywhere else.
- **Wordmark.** "MealDrop", one word, camelCase, set in the display face (Montserrat) with
  normal tracking (the wordmark deliberately drops the tight heading tracking). Never the
  body face, never all-caps.
- **The shine.** The logo can play a staggered "shine" — each shape briefly brightens toward
  `#9FF4F1` (lime `#EFFFBB` in dark), 400ms ease-in, rippling ear-to-ear. Opt-in and
  celebratory; also fires on hover. It belongs to the brand mark — don't imitate it on
  ordinary UI. Sizing: compact by default (24px), large on hero/marketing surfaces (75–150px).
- **Appetite appeal.** The food is the star: real, bright food photography does the
  tempting so the chrome doesn't have to. Character moments — the chomping "Looking for
  some food…" spinner, illustrated empty and error states — carry the personality.
- **Voice.** Full warmth, wink included:

| Moment         | Say this                                   | Not this                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Primary CTA    | View all restaurants                       | VIEW ALL RESTAURANTS / Click here     |
| Empty category | This is not the food you're looking for.   | No results found.                     |
| 500 error      | Our bad, something went wrong on our side. | Error 500: Internal Server Error      |
| 404            | This page doesn't exist, keep looking.     | The requested resource was not found. |
| Loading        | Looking for some food…                     | Loading…                              |
| Success        | Order confirmed!                           | Transaction complete.                 |

### DropBoard

The back office for restaurant partners — menus, live orders, payouts. Same design
language, quieter register: partners are working, not browsing.

- **Wordmark only.** "DropBoard", one word, camelCase, set in the display face with normal
  tracking. There is no DropBoard mascot; teal appears only in the wordmark and the
  loading spinner.
- **Denser, still calm.** Tables, filters, and numbers are the content here. Chrome stays
  tight and grey per BR-10; density comes from the type scale's small end, never from
  shrinking spacing below the system.
- **Playfulness dialed down, not off.** Empty states may keep a light touch ("No orders
  yet — enjoy the quiet."), but anything near money or order state is plain and exact
  ("Payout of €1,240.50 scheduled for Friday."). No jokes in error states that block work.
- **Same bones.** Focus ring, soft-green "good news" moments (e.g. a completed payout),
  red for errors only, sentence case throughout — all identical to MealDrop. A partner
  moving between the products should feel one company built both.

## Rules index

Stable IDs for automated and manual review. `BR` rules are the twelve brand principles above;
`DS` rules are design-system hygiene. `DS-01`–`DS-04` are the general usage rules that apply
to every component in this Storybook — use semantic `--ds-*` tokens, reach for an existing
component before building new, never introduce colors outside the palette, and pick the
semantically right component for the job.

| ID    | Rule                                                                                                       |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BR-01 | Grey does the work; color marks moments.                                                                   |
| BR-02 | The accents are surfaces, not ink.                                                                         |
| BR-03 | The brand color belongs to brand surfaces (in MealDrop, the mascot), not the interface.                    |
| BR-04 | Buttons are body text with a background.                                                                   |
| BR-05 | Dark mode re-decides roles; it doesn't invert.                                                             |
| BR-06 | Elevation means floating, never important.                                                                 |
| BR-07 | Hierarchy is size, not color.                                                                              |
| BR-08 | Sentence case, enforced by CSS.                                                                            |
| BR-09 | One focus ring, everywhere.                                                                                |
| BR-10 | Chrome is tight, content is airy.                                                                          |
| BR-11 | Radius follows floatiness, not size.                                                                       |
| BR-12 | Motion is brief, functional, and ease-in.                                                                  |
| DS-01 | Use semantic `--ds-*` tokens, never raw values or palette vars.                                            |
| DS-02 | Reach for an existing component before building new.                                                       |
| DS-03 | Never introduce colors outside the palette.                                                                |
| DS-04 | Pick the semantically right component for the job (e.g. radio group for exclusive choice, not checkboxes). |
